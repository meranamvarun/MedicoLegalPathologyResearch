# Study Protocol

## Title

**Diagnostic Errors in Pathology and Their Medico-Legal Consequences: A Mixed Legal and Clinical Review of Indian Case Law**

## 1. Background and Problem Statement

Pathology occupies a central role in modern healthcare, serving as the foundation for diagnosis, prognosis, treatment planning, and disease surveillance. Decisions made by pathologists directly influence patient management across a wide range of medical specialties, particularly oncology, surgery, obstetrics and gynecology, internal medicine, and forensic medicine. Histopathological examination, cytological interpretation, fine-needle aspiration cytology (FNAC), laboratory diagnostics, and autopsy findings frequently form the basis upon which critical clinical decisions are made.

Despite the importance of pathology in patient care, diagnostic errors remain an unavoidable challenge in medical practice. Such errors may arise from specimen handling issues, interpretative mistakes, reporting delays, communication failures, inadequate documentation, or limitations inherent in diagnostic techniques. While not every diagnostic error constitutes negligence, certain errors may lead to significant patient harm, including delayed treatment, inappropriate interventions, disease progression, psychological distress, or death.

In India, increasing patient awareness, expanding access to healthcare services, and growing reliance on legal remedies have contributed to a rise in medico-legal claims involving healthcare professionals. Courts, Consumer Disputes Redressal Commissions, and other adjudicatory forums are increasingly called upon to determine whether adverse clinical outcomes resulted from negligent medical conduct or represent acceptable risks and limitations of medical practice. In doing so, judicial bodies apply legal principles such as duty of care, breach of duty, causation, standard of care, the Bolam test, expert testimony, and distinctions between negligence and mere errors of judgment.

Although medical negligence litigation has been widely studied in specialties such as surgery, obstetrics and gynecology, anesthesiology, and emergency medicine, pathology remains comparatively underexplored within Indian medico-legal scholarship. Existing literature primarily consists of isolated case reports, commentary articles, or general discussions of medical negligence. There is a lack of systematic research examining how Indian courts evaluate diagnostic errors in pathology, what forms of alleged pathology-related negligence most commonly arise, which legal principles are applied, and what factors influence judicial outcomes.

This gap in knowledge is significant for several reasons. First, pathology-related disputes often involve complex questions of diagnostic interpretation that differ substantially from procedural or treatment-related negligence. Second, judicial expectations regarding diagnostic accuracy, documentation, reporting timelines, communication responsibilities, and quality assurance in pathology remain insufficiently understood. Third, identifying recurring causes of litigation may assist pathologists, laboratories, hospitals, and policymakers in strengthening risk management practices and improving patient safety.

Accordingly, there is a need for a comprehensive examination of reported Indian judicial decisions involving alleged diagnostic errors in pathology. Such an analysis would not only provide insight into patterns of litigation and judicial reasoning but also bridge the gap between legal standards and practical pathology practice.

This study proposes to undertake a mixed legal and clinical review of Indian case law involving pathology-related diagnostic errors. By combining legal analysis with clinical interpretation, the study aims to identify common allegations, evaluate judicial approaches to determining negligence, assess litigation outcomes, and derive practical lessons for pathologists and healthcare institutions. The findings are expected to contribute to medico-legal scholarship, pathology education, risk management strategies, and evidence-based policy discussions concerning professional accountability in diagnostic medicine.

## 2. Aim

To analyze reported Indian judicial decisions involving diagnostic errors in pathology and evaluate their medico-legal consequences through integrated legal and clinical review.

## 3. Objectives

1. To identify and compile reported Indian judicial decisions involving allegations of diagnostic errors by pathologists, pathology laboratories, or pathology departments.
2. To classify the nature of alleged diagnostic errors, including:
   - histopathology interpretation errors;
   - cytology interpretation errors;
   - FNAC-related errors;
   - delayed diagnosis;
   - reporting delays;
   - specimen handling errors;
   - documentation deficiencies;
   - communication failures; and
   - autopsy-related disputes.
3. To determine the distribution of cases across judicial forums, including:
   - Consumer Commissions;
   - High Courts;
   - Supreme Court of India; and
   - Criminal Courts.
4. To evaluate litigation outcomes and determine:
   - proportion of cases decided in favor of plaintiffs;
   - proportion of cases decided in favor of defendants;
   - compensation awarded; and
   - trends over time.
5. To identify legal principles commonly applied by courts in pathology-related negligence disputes.
6. To examine factors associated with findings of liability, including documentation quality, expert evidence, reporting delays, communication failures, and deviations from accepted standards of practice.
7. To assess, from a clinical pathology perspective, whether alleged diagnostic errors reflected negligence, system failures, diagnostic limitations, or acceptable differences in professional interpretation.
8. To develop practical recommendations aimed at reducing medico-legal risk and improving diagnostic quality in pathology practice.

## 4. Research Questions

1. What types of pathology-related diagnostic errors most frequently lead to litigation in India?
2. Which judicial forums most commonly adjudicate pathology-related medico-legal disputes?
3. What proportion of cases result in findings of negligence against pathologists or pathology institutions?
4. What legal principles are most frequently relied upon by courts when assessing pathology-related negligence?
5. What clinical, procedural, or documentation-related factors contribute to adverse judicial findings?
6. How do courts distinguish between negligent conduct and acceptable diagnostic error in pathology practice?
7. What lessons can be derived from judicial decisions to improve patient safety, diagnostic accuracy, and medico-legal risk management?

## 5. Significance of the Study

This study will be among the first systematic reviews of Indian case law specifically focused on pathology-related diagnostic errors. By integrating legal and clinical perspectives, it seeks to provide a comprehensive understanding of how diagnostic disputes are evaluated in the Indian legal system. The findings may assist pathologists, medical educators, healthcare administrators, legal professionals, policymakers, and researchers in developing safer diagnostic practices and reducing future medico-legal conflicts.

## 6. Methodology

The methodology is structured as a systematic, mixed legal and clinical review. It includes source-specific searching, provenance logging, eligibility screening, deduplication, structured legal and clinical coding, literature citation chasing, descriptive quantitative summaries, doctrinal legal analysis, clinical pathology interpretation, and thematic synthesis. Detailed operational procedures are provided in `docs/data_collection_plan.md` and `docs/literature_review_tracking_plan.md`.

## 7. Study Design

This is a mixed legal and clinical review of reported Indian case law. The study combines:

1. **Doctrinal legal analysis** of judicial reasoning, legal principles, forum outcomes, causation analysis, expert evidence, and compensation decisions.
2. **Structured case-level data extraction** to support descriptive quantitative summaries of case characteristics and outcomes.
3. **Clinical pathology review** to classify alleged diagnostic errors and assess whether the events appear to represent negligence, diagnostic limitations, system failures, or acceptable professional disagreement.
4. **Narrative and thematic synthesis** of recurring medico-legal issues, risk factors, and practice recommendations.

## 8. Population and Unit of Analysis

The primary unit of analysis is a reported Indian judicial or quasi-judicial decision involving an alleged pathology-related diagnostic error. Where multiple decisions arise from the same dispute, each decision may be tracked separately for procedural history, while linked under a common appellate-history identifier for dispute-level analysis.

## 9. Data Sources

### 9.1 Case-law sources

The study will search multiple sources because pathology-related disputes may be inconsistently indexed:

1. Supreme Court of India judgments and orders.
2. High Court judgment portals and public case-law repositories.
3. National Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission decisions.
4. State and District Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission decisions where available.
5. Indian legal databases available to the research team, including public or institutionally licensed platforms.
6. Secondary citations from retrieved judgments, medico-legal literature, and legal commentary.

### 9.2 Literature sources

The literature review will use biomedical, public-health, legal, and medico-legal sources to identify background scholarship, case commentary, and citation leads. Candidate sources include PubMed/MEDLINE, Google Scholar, IndMED or other Indian biomedical indexes, institutional library discovery tools, law journal databases, and legal commentary platforms.

## 10. Search Strategy

Searches will combine pathology terms, diagnostic-error terms, legal terms, and India-specific jurisdiction terms. Detailed case-law search terms are maintained in `config/search_strategy.yml`, and literature search terms are maintained in `config/literature_search_strategy.yml`.

Each executed search should be logged with the source, query, filters, date searched, number of hits, export path, searcher, and notes. Case-law searches should be logged in `data/metadata/search_log_template.csv`; literature searches should be logged in `data/metadata/literature_search_log_template.csv`.

## 11. Eligibility Criteria

### 11.1 Case-law inclusion criteria

Include a decision if all criteria are satisfied:

1. The decision is from an Indian judicial or quasi-judicial forum.
2. The matter concerns alleged diagnostic error, reporting failure, specimen handling problem, or pathology/autopsy-related dispute.
3. The decision contains enough factual detail to classify the alleged pathology issue and outcome.
4. The case involves patient care, forensic/autopsy determination, or clinical decision-making influenced by pathology or laboratory evidence.

### 11.2 Case-law exclusion criteria

Exclude a decision if any criterion applies:

1. The matter is about general hospital negligence without an identifiable pathology or laboratory diagnostic issue.
2. The reference to laboratory testing is incidental and not part of the negligence allegation or judicial reasoning.
3. The matter is a duplicate report of a case already included at a later or more authoritative appellate stage, unless multiple stages are required for outcome tracking.
4. The document lacks sufficient detail after reasonable attempts to retrieve the full judgment.
5. The matter is non-Indian or only discusses foreign case law.

### 11.3 Literature inclusion criteria

Include literature records if they discuss pathology/laboratory medicine or diagnostic error in a medico-legal context, Indian medical negligence law relevant to diagnostic error, Indian cases involving diagnostic or laboratory issues, or clinical and quality-assurance guidance relevant to interpreting pathology error mechanisms.

## 12. Data Collection Workflow

1. Run source-specific searches using the configured case-law and literature strategies.
2. Save raw case search exports to `data/raw/search_results/` and raw literature exports to `data/raw/literature/`.
3. Save full judgments or orders to `data/raw/cases/` using stable, descriptive filenames.
4. Enter candidate legal decisions into `data/metadata/case_inventory_template.csv`.
5. Enter candidate literature records into `data/metadata/literature_inventory_template.csv`.
6. Conduct title/snippet screening, followed by full-text eligibility screening.
7. Deduplicate cases by party names, citation, decision date, forum, and factual narrative.
8. Link appellate histories and related decisions under common identifiers.
9. Extract case-level legal and clinical fields into the coding template.
10. Extract literature-level concepts, cited cases, and methodological/background relevance into the literature extraction template.
11. Cross-check literature citation leads against the case inventory to identify additional eligible decisions.

## 14. Coding and Reviewer Process

Legally and clinically complex fields should be dual-coded. At minimum, one legal reviewer and one pathology or clinical reviewer should independently code each eligible case. Disagreements should be reconciled through consensus, with decisions documented in the audit trail.

Recommended coding categories include:

1. **Screening status:** eligible, excluded, unclear.
2. **Error type:** histopathology, cytology, FNAC, delay, specimen handling, documentation, communication, autopsy, mixed, other.
3. **Outcome:** plaintiff success, defendant success, partial success, remand, settlement/withdrawn, unclear.
4. **Negligence finding:** negligence found, negligence not found, not decided, unclear.
5. **Clinical interpretation:** negligent error, non-negligent diagnostic limitation, system/process failure, acceptable professional disagreement, unclear.

## 15. Analysis Plan

### 15.1 Descriptive analysis

The study will summarize:

1. number of eligible cases by year;
2. forum distribution;
3. state or jurisdiction distribution;
4. pathology domains involved;
5. alleged error categories;
6. claimant and defendant types;
7. outcomes by forum and error category; and
8. compensation amounts, where awarded.

### 15.2 Legal analysis

Legal analysis will identify and synthesize:

1. duty of care and standard-of-care reasoning;
2. application of Bolam or related professional-standard tests;
3. treatment of expert evidence;
4. causation findings;
5. distinctions between negligence and error of judgment;
6. reasoning around documentation, communication, reporting delay, and institutional responsibility; and
7. factors that appear associated with liability or dismissal.

### 15.3 Clinical pathology analysis

Clinical review will assess whether alleged errors appear to reflect:

1. interpretive diagnostic error;
2. specimen sampling limitation;
3. specimen handling or identification failure;
4. reporting or communication failure;
5. documentation deficiency;
6. autopsy or forensic interpretation dispute;
7. system/process failure; or
8. acceptable difference in professional interpretation.

### 15.4 Literature synthesis

The literature review will synthesize background scholarship, prior case commentary, medico-legal doctrines, diagnostic-error frameworks, and clinical risk-management recommendations. Literature citation leads will be reconciled against the legal case inventory.

## 16. Quality Assurance

1. Preserve raw judgments, case search exports, and literature exports without manual editing.
2. Maintain complete search logs for every database and source searched.
3. Document inclusion and exclusion decisions for both cases and literature records.
4. Use stable case identifiers and linked appellate-history identifiers.
5. Deduplicate cases and literature records using transparent criteria.
6. Re-code a sample of eligible cases to assess consistency between reviewers.
7. Keep raw, processed, and analysis-ready datasets separate.
8. Document deviations from the protocol in project notes or future protocol amendments.

## 17. Ethical, Legal, and Data Management Considerations

The study will rely on publicly available judicial decisions or institutionally licensed databases used within their terms. Raw source material should not be redistributed if license terms prohibit redistribution. Published outputs should avoid unnecessary personal identifiers beyond what is required for legal citation and analysis. All derived datasets should preserve provenance, source references, and screening/coding audit trails.

## 18. Expected Outputs

Expected outputs include:

1. a compiled dataset of eligible Indian pathology-related diagnostic-error decisions;
2. descriptive tables of forums, outcomes, error types, compensation, and time trends;
3. thematic legal analysis of judicial principles and reasoning;
4. clinical synthesis of diagnostic-error mechanisms and risk-management lessons;
5. tracked literature review outputs and citation-lead tables; and
6. practical recommendations for pathologists, laboratories, hospitals, educators, and policymakers.
